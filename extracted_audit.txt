

WEBSITE AUDIT &POSITIONING STRATEGY REPORT
Sri Lanka – France Business Council (SLFBC)
Audit target: https://www.slfbc.lk/index.php

Prepared: 24 September 2026

Scope: content architecture, positioning, UI/UX, functionality, trust, mobile readiness, SEO fundamentals and modernization opportunities.
This audit evaluates the requested www.slfbc.lk site.

Executive Summary
Key Finding: The current SLFBC website communicates the council's basic purpose, membership and contact information, but its presentation and information architecture feel closer to a legacy institutional website than a modern bilateral business council platform. The strongest opportunity is not simply a visual redesign: the site should make the Council's value to Sri Lanka–France business relationships immediately clear, while making membership, events, partnerships, trade/investment opportunities and council leadership easy to discover.
The requested site currently contains a useful navigation structure—About, Membership, News & Events, Country and Contact—but several pages rely on older PHP URLs, legacy external links, inconsistent contact details and content that appears dated. The homepage also has limited conversion-oriented messaging beyond informational content.
Primary Priorities
Modernize the homepage around the Council's bilateral business value: connect, facilitate, promote and represent.
Create clear pathways for Members, Prospective Members, French Businesses, Sri Lankan Businesses and Institutional Partners.
Bring leadership and committee information up to date and present it with consistent professional portraits and role information.
Consolidate news/event content into the SLFBC domain instead of relying on legacy external event destinations.
Standardize contact details, social links, footer information and copyright/version information.
Improve mobile responsiveness, spacing, typography, image treatment and accessibility.
Introduce stronger calls to action such as Join the Council, Explore Members, View Upcoming Events and Contact the Secretariat.
Create a more useful France / Sri Lanka country-information section with business-relevant content rather than generic country information.
Overall Website Direction
Recommended direction: A premium, institutional, bilateral-business platform combining the credibility of the Ceylon Chamber of Commerce with a modern international-business presentation. The site should feel authoritative and current without becoming overly corporate or promotional.

Supporting line: The Sri Lanka–France Business Council connects businesses, institutions and investors to strengthen commercial relationships between the two countries.
Primary CTA: Join the Council

Homepage Sections
Hero: bilateral business proposition + two clear CTAs.

Featured Members: selected member logos with Explore All Members CTA.

Homepage Content Issue
The current homepage uses a Vision, Mission and Services block, but these statements are abstract. They should be retained as supporting content rather than the main conversion message. The homepage should answer three questions within the first screen: What is SLFBC? Who is it for? What can a visitor do next?












2. About, Governance & Leadership
About the Council
The existing About page contains the Council's history and objectives. These are valuable institutional assets and should remain, but the copy should be broken into shorter sections with clear headings and visual anchors.
Who We Are
Our History — Founded in 2004
Our Role within the Ceylon Chamber of Commerce
Our Objectives
Sri Lanka–France Business Relationship
Our Network & Institutional Connections
Leadership / Committee Page
The committee page should clearly state the applicable year, for example 'Executive Committee 2025/26', and avoid mixing historical committee years with current information. The current indexed slfbc.lk version lists a 2025/26 committee, including a President, Senior Vice President, Vice Presidents, committee members and invited institutional representatives.
Recommended presentation: consistent professional headshots, name, role, organization and a short 1–2 line professional profile. Avoid displaying personal phone numbers or private contact information unless the Council has explicitly approved publication.
Ambassador Message
The Ambassador Message should be visually elevated as an institutional message page, with the Ambassador's name, title, date/year and a high-quality official portrait. The page should be reviewed annually so that the displayed Ambassador and message remain current.

4. Membership Experience
Membership is one of the strongest assets of the website. The current member page contains a substantial directory of companies, including international and Sri Lankan organizations across logistics, manufacturing, tourism, finance, ICT and other sectors.
However, the current presentation is essentially a long text list. This makes the directory difficult to scan and does not communicate the scale or diversity of the network visually.
Recommended Member Directory
Search by company name.
Filter by sector.
Filter by Sri Lankan / French / international organization where applicable.
Display company logo, company name and sector.
Optional company profile page or external website link.
Feature selected members on the homepage.
Show a clear 'Become a Member' CTA.
Membership Application
The existing application page asks applicants to submit a business registration certificate, company profile, audited accounts for two years, applicable fees and two membership recommendations. The current page therefore contains useful operational information but should be redesigned into a clear application journey.
Step 1 — Confirm eligibility
Step 2 — Prepare required documents
Step 3 — Complete application
Step 4 — Submit to the Secretariat
Step 5 — Committee review / membership confirmation
The application fee/subscription figures should be reviewed and dated before publication because membership charges can change.

Conclusion
SLFBC already has the most important raw ingredients for a strong institutional website: a defined bilateral mission, a long operating history, a substantial member network, committee leadership, event activity and a relationship with the Ceylon Chamber of Commerce. The main requirement is to turn these assets into a modern, current and easier-to-use digital platform.
The redesign should therefore focus on three outcomes: first, make the Council's bilateral business value immediately understandable; second, make the network and activities easy to explore; and third, make every important action—joining, attending, contacting, finding a member or learning about an opportunity—simple to complete.
